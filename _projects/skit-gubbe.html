Skit Gubbe started as my final school project. I worked on it for 3 months and have kept going since I graduated, polishing and refining the game, which I’m still doing.

I was really into cards and card games at the time and wanted to play Skit Gubbe digitally. When I searched, I couldn’t find a digital version of the game, so I was inspired to make it myself. It turned into a project I’m really proud of.

Q-learning AI opponent

In Skit Gubbe I’ve experimented with using machine learning to develop an AI opponent. I first made a simple opponent that only played the lowest card possible, but later I started looking into Q-learning: a type of reinforcement learning where the AI learns by trial and error while playing against itself.

It plays the game over and over and gradually figures out which actions tend to lead to winning. It does that by saving states to a Q-table, which it then references while playing. If it hasn’t seen an exact position (state) before, it saves it to the Q-table, along with whether its actions were good or bad, which it decides through a reward system.

Training simulation

To teach the Q-learning AI, I used an empty scene in Unity to run millions of simulations where it plays itself and learns the states that are then saved to the Q-table.

Training starts with the AI playing randomly, so it learns a lot of states early on. As the epsilon value drops, it plays randomly less and less and refers to the states in the Q-table instead, which makes its win rate go up throughout the simulation.
